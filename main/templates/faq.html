  • To purchase real estate in Dubai, buyers generally require:

    • Passport copy
    • Emirates ID (for UAE resident)
    • Visa copy (if applicable)
    • Proof of funds or bank approval (for financed purchases)
    • Signed Sales Purchase Agreement (SPA) for off-plan properties
    • Memorandum of Understanding (Form F) for resale property transactions

  • When buying property in Dubai, buyers should consider several additional costs, including:

    • 4% Dubai Land Department (DLD) fee — Government registration fee
    • Real estate agency commission — Usually around 2% of the property value
    • Trustee office fees — Required for property registration procedures
    • Mortgage-related charges — Applicable if financing is used
    • Service charges — Annual maintenance fees that vary depending on the building, community, and property type

  • All property transactions in Dubai are registered with the Dubai Land Department through authorized trustee offices, where the buyer receives an official Title Deed confirming ownership.

  • A Title Deed is the legal ownership document issued by DLD, confirming your property ownership in Dubai. It's the most important document in any real estate transaction.

  • Form F, also known as the Memorandum of Understanding (MoU), outlines the terms agreed between buyer and seller in a Dubai real estate transaction, including price, payment schedule, and conditions.

  • An off-plan property is purchased directly from a developer before project completion, often offering flexible payment plans and competitive pricing. Buyers benefit from potential appreciation before completion.

  • Yes, buying off-plan property in Dubai is considered safe when purchasing from approved developers and registered projects. Dubai’s real estate sector is regulated by the Dubai Land Department (DLD) and RERA. Buyer payments for registered off-plan projects are protected through escrow accounts, where funds are released according to construction progress.

  • An escrow account is a government-regulated account where buyer funds are held securely. Amounts are released to developers based on construction progress milestones, ensuring buyer protection.

  • Tenants typically need:

    • Passport copy
    • Emirates ID
    • Visa copy
    • Security deposit (5% for unfurnished, 10% for furnished)
    • Ejari registration (mandatory rental contract)

  • Ejari is a mandatory rental contract registration system regulated by RERA that protects both landlords and tenants in Dubai. It provides legal documentation of the tenancy agreement.

  • In Dubai, security deposits are commonly based on the annual rental amount. Typically, landlords may request around 5% of the annual rent for unfurnished properties and around 10% for furnished properties. The deposit is usually refundable at the end of the tenancy, subject to property condition and the terms of the rental agreement.
